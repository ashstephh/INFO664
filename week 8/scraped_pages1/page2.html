Introducing Charlotte…

What can the Museum Data Service learn from someone working with data in the NHS?

Posted on 5 October 2026

We’re delighted to have Charlotte Sadler with the Museum Data Service (MDS) for a two-month placement spanning September and October 2026. Over to Charlotte to introduce herself… 

I have joined the MDS for my “flexi placement” from the NHS Graduate Management Training Scheme. Placements allow trainees to work outside of the NHS for 2 months to pick up different skills and perspectives. My background before joining the scheme was in Art History and Social Anthropology, and I was really interested in learning more about the intersection of my different skillsets and experiences. I came to the MDS as I wanted to learn how art and material culture can align with data. 

So far, I have found many familiar challenges that exist in the NHS also exist within the museum sector. The biggest challenge we often talk about in the NHS is interoperability between different systems, and how we can securely share patient records in one centralised space. MDS is actively answering that question for the Museum Sector; through mapping each museum’s record structure to Spectrum fields and a shared digital platform, MDS can create one centralised database of objects throughout the UK. 

My first placement in the NHS was within the Data Quality team, and part of the plan for my time with MDS is to utilise this skill set; where I will be investigating the different types of Data Quality issues that impact data collation in the MDS. 

Recently MDS broke the 8 million record mark, but the data can be hard for people to navigate. Some of this stems from the difference in data architecture within museum records, but there are other reasons too: whether the data is complete, whether the data is consistent through one museum’s record, if information exists for each field of each record within a museum. In some cases, there can be variation of quality of records within one museum, so amalgamating many together can highlight new record-keeping discrepancies. 

The other part of my time at MDS will be looking at road mapping ways to provide more usable records for MDS users. If you’d like to support this work, please don’t hesitate to reach out at support(at)museumdata.uk
